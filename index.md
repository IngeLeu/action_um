# ACTION Lab
## Action, Cognition, and Targeted Intervention of Oscillatory Networks

At the ACTION Lab, we study how dynamic brain networks allow us to act, decide, remember, and adapt. We are particularly interested in how brain rhythms, neurochemistry, and brain–body interactions shape cognition and behaviour.

Our research is inspired by clinical questions, but rooted in fundamental neuroscience. We start from challenges observed in neurological and psychiatric disorders, such as Parkinson’s disease and obsessive-compulsive disorder, and use them to ask mechanistic questions about how the brain controls behaviour. By combining behavioural tasks, EEG, magnetic resonance spectroscopy, brain stimulation, and computational approaches, we aim to uncover the principles by which oscillatory brain networks support flexible cognition and action.

A central goal of the lab is to translate this mechanistic knowledge back toward clinical applications. We use targeted and multimodal approaches, including electrical stimulation in 7T MRI/MRS environments and closed-loop EEG–tACS, to test how brain rhythms causally contribute to behaviour and how they may eventually be modulated more effectively.
